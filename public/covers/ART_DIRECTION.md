# Genre cover art direction — one language per skin

Spec for replacing the 159 stock photographs in `public/covers/` with **generated genre covers that
match the active skin's own art style**, so a genre tile stops being a random photo and starts looking
like it belongs to the interface it is shown in.

> **Skin count.** `src/types/skin.ts` and `src/data/skins.ts` define **six** skins —
> `default`, `minimal`, `comic`, `soviet`, `sovietYears`, `pixel` — and
> `src/test/skinSystem.test.tsx` asserts exactly those six, in that order. There is no seventh skin in
> the codebase (`legacySkin.css` / `panelSkin.css` under `src/mobile/skins/` are shared mobile
> internals, not catalogue skins). This spec therefore covers **six** art directions, not seven.

> **Genre count.** `public/covers/` holds **159** `<genre-id>.jpg` files, not 160. The full id list is
> in the appendix.

---

## 0. The rules that apply to every skin

These are non-negotiable across all six directions, because they come from how the images are used, not
from taste.

1. **Square, 1:1, tile-first.** Every cover is displayed as a small square tile (and scaled up in the
   player). The composition must read at 120 px: one dominant shape, one clear focal point, high
   contrast between subject and ground. No fine detail that only survives at full resolution.
2. **No text, no lettering, no logos, no watermarks, no signatures, no UI, no frames.** Several skins
   *are* typographic (comic, soviet, sovietYears, pixel) — but the lettering is the skin's job, applied
   by CSS. A cover carrying its own words would collide with the UI's type. Render *abstraction of
   type*, not type.
3. **No depictions of real artists, real album covers, real brands, or recognisable people.** An
   anonymous silhouette, a hand, a crowd in the far distance are fine; a face that reads as a specific
   person is not.
4. **The genre's musical identity must be visible in the image.** Instruments, a club or a room, a
   landscape, a machine, a motion — something that says *this* music, not "music" in general. A cover
   that could be swapped for any other genre has failed, even if it is beautiful.
5. **Premium, not stock.** These replace scraped Lorem Picsum photos. The bar is a high-end record
   sleeve or a print the user would hang on a wall. Ban the stock-photo vocabulary explicitly:
   *clipart, flat vector icon, generic 3D blob, floating geometric shapes on a gradient, stock
   corporate imagery, plastic toy render, cheap AI gloss.*
6. **Keep the skin's palette, in the skin's proportions.** Each direction below names a ground, an ink
   and an accent taken from that skin's actual CSS tokens. The ground dominates (≈70 %), the ink
   carries structure (≈20 %), the accent is spent on one thing (≈10 %) — the same discipline the UI
   itself uses. A cover for `minimal` may not be colourful; a cover for `comic` may not be tasteful
   grey.
7. **Common negative prompt** (append to every generation):
   `text, letters, words, typography, logo, watermark, signature, UI, screenshot, frame, border,
   photograph of a real person, celebrity, album cover, brand, clipart, flat vector icon, stock photo,
   generic corporate imagery, floating geometric shapes, plastic 3D render, oversaturated HDR,
   low detail, blurry, jpeg artifacts`
8. **Determinism.** One stable seed per (skin, genre) pair, derived from the ids, so a cover can be
   regenerated byte-comparably and never re-rolls between builds. Suggested:
   `seed = 1000 + 137 * skinIndex + fnv1a(genreId) % 1000`.
9. **Output.** PNG from the generator, converted to **JPEG q92 at 1024×1024**, written to
   `public/covers/<skin-id>/<genre-id>.jpg`. `src/mobile/genreArt.ts#genreCoverUrl()` is the resolution
   hook the parent agent will re-point at the skin directory; this spec adds files only and changes no
   code.

## 1. `default` — "aurora in glass"

**Swatches:** ground `#0b0b14` (desktop `rgb(10 11 13)`), ink `#f3f3f9`, accent `#5eead4`.
Secondary: rose `#fb7185`, violet `#a78bfa`, green `#4ade80`.

**Visual language.** The app's flagship dark theme: a cool near-black indigo ground with soft luminous
colour, translucency and depth — the palette doc calls it "a cool, near-black ground … colour now comes
from the module accent and from each genre's own art", and `genreArt.ts` already encodes the house look
as an *aurora*: violet → pink → teal over deep navy. So the medium is **luminous**: a high-end 3D render
or long-exposure photographic light, not print. Think volumetric haze, a slow shutter, glass and water
catching coloured light. Light should *emit* from within the scene (a glow, a beam, a bloom) rather than
fall on it from outside. The accent mint-teal `#5eead4` is the one saturated note — a laser line, a lit
edge, a signal; violet and rose `#fb7185` are the supporting aurora wash. Composition is centred and
symmetric-ish, deep-focus, the subject emerging from darkness with a soft falloff to a near-black edge so
the tile melts into the app's ground. Texture: fine film grain over smooth gradients, bokeh, caustics,
subsurface glow — glossy and modern, the visual equivalent of the shell's blurred glass panels. **Avoid:**
daylight, flat fills, hard black outlines, visible paper, warm browns, retro kitsch, anything matte or
printed, and any neon that reads 1980s rather than present-day luxury.

**Prompt template**
```
High-end cinematic 3D render album cover, square, subject: {GENRE_SUBJECT}, artistically expressing the
sound of {GENRE} — {GENRE_CHARACTER}. Deep near-black indigo ground (#0b0b14) fading to pure dark at the
edges, lit only by emitted light: mint-teal (#5eead4) as the single sharp accent, an aurora wash of
soft violet (#a78bfa) and rose (#fb7185) behind. Volumetric haze, bloom, long-exposure light trails,
glass and water caustics, deep focus, generous negative space, subtle film grain over smooth gradients,
premium and quietly futuristic. No text.
```

## 2. `minimal` — "one blue line on paper"

**Swatches:** ground `#fafafa` (card `#ffffff`, panel2 `#f4f4f6`), ink `#141414`, accent `#2358e6`.

**Visual language.** The stylesheet's own words: paper-white, black ink, and *restraint is a set of
decisions* — "hairlines instead of borders with weight, no shadows or gradients at all, one typeface,
tight tracking, and the accent spent on one thing at a time." The medium is therefore **editorial print
and Swiss graphic design**: offset litho on uncoated white stock, or a hard-edged screen print in two
inks. Flat. Absolutely no gradient, no glow, no bevel, no shadow — if the render has a soft falloff
anywhere it is wrong. Composition is asymmetric but strictly gridded, with a large amount of empty
paper as the actual subject; one geometric element (a circle, a bar, a hairline that runs off the edge,
a single arc) carries the whole image, placed with intention and never centred by default. Everything is
ink black `#141414` except **one** element in ultramarine `#2358e6` — the accent is spent once, the way
the UI spends it on one thing at a time. Line weight is a hairline, 1–2 px at tile scale, perfectly
crisp. Where the music needs to appear (a waveform, a string, a key, a speaker cone), it appears as a
reduced geometric abstraction — a single continuous line, a measured bar, a repeated tick — never as an
illustration. Texture: the faintest uncoated-paper tooth and nothing else. **Avoid:** colour beyond the
one blue, gradients, shadows, blur, glow, texture overlays, decorative noise, 3D, photographic realism,
fussy detail, symmetry for its own sake.

**Prompt template**
```
Swiss editorial graphic design, minimal offset print on uncoated white paper, square album cover,
subject: {GENRE_SUBJECT} reduced to pure geometry, expressing the sound of {GENRE} — {GENRE_CHARACTER}.
Flat paper-white ground (#fafafa), ink-black (#141414) hairline geometry, strict invisible modular
grid, very generous empty space, asymmetric and deliberate placement. Exactly one element in
ultramarine blue (#2358e6); everything else black on white. No gradients, no shadows, no glow, no blur,
no 3D, no texture beyond uncoated paper tooth. One continuous hairline abstraction of the music. No text.
```

## 3. `comic` — "cheap 70s press run"

**Swatches:** ground newsprint `#f4e9d2` (panel `#fbf4e2`, panel2 `#f2e3c0`), ink `#111014`,
accent process cyan `#0b6e7f`. Plates: magenta `#c4006e`, red `#c50034`, orange `#f08a1e`,
green `#00702f`, violet `#5b2a9e`.

**Visual language.** The sheet is a four-colour comic press that ran hot: the stylesheet describes
"newsprint, ink outlines, Ben-Day dots, flat spot plates, cyan and magenta a hair out of register", hard
zero-blur offset shadows, and a result that is "the brownish-orange misregistration of a cheap 70s comic
run" rather than clean modern neon. So the medium is **vintage comic printing**: heavy black ink
keylines of varying weight, flat unshaded spot colour inside them, and a visible **Ben-Day halftone dot
screen** over everything, with the colour plates deliberately offset by 1–3 px so edges fringe magenta on
one side and cyan on the other. Light is graphic, not physical: flat fills, no rendering, no gradients
inside shapes; form is described by outline and by dot density alone. Composition is *loud* and
off-balance: a big, dynamic, low-angle subject bursting out of the frame, speed lines, impact starburst,
radiating action lines, the panel cropped hard on the interesting part. Colour is warm newsprint cream
with saturated spot plates — cyan, magenta, orange, green — used flat and sparingly, like a printer
rationing ink. Where the music shows up it is *heroic and physical*: a guitar swung like a weapon, a
drum kit exploding, a singer mid-shout as an anonymous silhouette. Texture: paper fibre, slight ink
misregistration, a faint rosette in the dot screens, cream paper stock with soft worn edges. **Avoid:**
digital gradients, airbrush, photorealism, 3D render, clean vector flatness (this is *rough* print), any
text or speech balloons, and any modern "Spider-Verse" gloss — the target is a *cheap* 1970s run.

**Prompt template**
```
Vintage 1970s comic book printing, four-colour press on warm newsprint cream paper (#f4e9d2), square
album cover, subject: {GENRE_SUBJECT} drawn bold and heroic, expressing the sound of {GENRE} —
{GENRE_CHARACTER}. Heavy black ink (#111014) keylines of varied weight, flat unshaded spot colour, large
visible Ben-Day halftone dot screens, colour plates (process cyan #0b6e7f, magenta #c4006e, orange
#f08a1e) deliberately offset 1-3 px out of register so edges fringe. Dynamic low-angle composition,
radiating action lines and speed lines, hard zero-blur black drop shadows, ink bleed into paper fibre,
slightly worn edges. Loud and energetic, limited ink palette, no rendering or gradients inside shapes.
No text, no speech balloons, no lettering.
```

## 4. `soviet` — "pressed steel and brass"

**Swatches:** ground gunmetal `#1b1e21` (panel `#22262a`, panel2 `#2b3034`, surface `#2f3337`),
ink bone `#ede4cc`, accent brass `#e6c766`.

**Visual language.** The stylesheet calls it the heavy-industry skin: "a stamped-steel panel has square
corners, a 2 px edge, an inset bevel that reads as pressed metal, wide stencilled capital letters, and
controls that physically depress", and it paints "a faint vertical brush, so the ground is metal rather
than a flat fill." The medium is therefore **machined metal and industrial print**: a brushed-steel
plate, a cast housing, a stencil sprayed onto metal, or a photogravure of a machine. Low-key, raking
light that catches the **vertical brush grain** of the steel and picks out a milled edge; the ground is
cold gunmetal, the highlights are bone-white `#ede4cc`, and **brass `#e6c766`** is the single warm note —
a rating plate, a rivet head, a gauge needle, a stamped numeral. Composition is frontal, symmetrical and
deliberately *engineered*: the subject centred like an assembly drawing or a technical plate, hard square
corners, visible fasteners, seams and a serial-plate geometry (but no readable numerals). Depth comes
from bevels, not from perspective. The music appears as **machinery**: a piston, a turbine, a valve, a
speaker as a driver cone, an amplifier as a heat-sink chassis, a sequencer as a stamped drum with
punched holes. Texture: brushed steel, cast pitting, chipped paint, oil sheen, fine oxidation in the
recesses, stencil overspray. **Avoid:** anything colourful or cheerful, brass used everywhere (it is one
accent), glossy plastic, rounded corners, soft bloom, digital gradients, neon, and the *red* of the
`sovietYears` skin — this palette has no red, only steel, bone and brass.

**Prompt template**
```
Heavy-industry machined-metal rendering, square album cover, subject: {GENRE_SUBJECT} built as
industrial machinery, expressing the sound of {GENRE} — {GENRE_CHARACTER}. Cold gunmetal ground
(#1b1e21, panels #22262a / #2b3034) with visible vertical brushed-steel grain, bone-white (#ede4cc)
raked highlights, brass (#e6c766) as the single warm accent on one rated part. Stamped-steel plates with
square corners, 2 px milled edges, inset bevels, rivets, seams, cast pitting and chipped paint, wide
stencilled capital shapes with no readable letters, technical-plate frontal composition, low-key
raking light, oil sheen, faint oxidation in the recesses. Cold, heavy, precise and premium. No text,
no numbers, no logos.
```

## 5. `sovietYears` — "agitprop on aged paper"

**Swatches:** ground aged paper `#f4f1e1` (panel `#ece7d6`, panel2 `#ded7bf`), ink `#111111`,
accent flag red `#cc0000`. Secondary: dark blue `#2c3e50`, deep red `#9e0b22`, green `#1c5c2e`.

**Visual language.** This is the constructivist poster, and the stylesheet states the recipe exactly:
"A propaganda poster, pasted up and printed too heavily. Two inks and one paper: flag red is `--d-accent`,
the dense black plate is `--d-ink`, aged paper is `--d-bg`. Nothing is soft — every gradient is
flattened, every glow is deleted … Tailwind's soft shadow scale is replaced with zero-blur plate offsets
(`10px 10px 0` on a panel, `6px 6px 0` on a card, `3px 3px 0` on a key)", with the heavy machine-cut
display face over a condensed news face, and a `skewX` shear that makes the type *lean*. So: **1920s
Soviet constructivist / agitprop lithography** — two flat inks, red and black, on coarse aged paper.
Composition is built on **aggressive diagonals** (the stylesheet's own skew), a strong red wedge or beam
cutting across the square, concentric or radiating geometry, hard overlapping flat shapes, and a heroic
figure or object seen as a **photomontage silhouette** or a severely reduced geometric icon. Red `#cc0000`
is a large *field*, not a spice — a third to a half of the image — with black `#111111` bearing the
structure and the aged paper `#f4f1e1` showing through as the third colour. Light is irrelevant; value
comes from flat mass. The music appears as **mass movement and industry**: a factory, a radio mast, a
crowd, a fist, a wheel, a loudspeaker horn, a gear train, a march — collective and monumental, never
individual. Texture: coarse paper fibre, misregistered flat ink, visible offset plate edges, ink that has
sunk into the stock, a slightly torn or pasted poster edge, and hard **zero-blur** black plate shadows.
**Avoid:** gradients, soft shadows, atmospheric perspective, photorealism, 3D, gloss, subtlety, greens or
blues as major colours, and any readable Cyrillic or Latin lettering (no lettering at all).

**Prompt template**
```
1920s Soviet constructivist propaganda poster, two flat inks on coarse aged paper, square album cover,
subject: {GENRE_SUBJECT} as a monumental photomontage silhouette or severely reduced geometric icon,
expressing the sound of {GENRE} — {GENRE_CHARACTER}. Aged paper ground (#f4f1e1) as the third colour,
flag red (#cc0000) used as a large flat field covering a third to half of the image, dense black
(#111111) carrying all structure. Aggressive diagonals, skewed and sheared geometry, a red wedge or beam
cutting across the square, radiating and concentric flat shapes, hard overlapping masses, zero-blur
plate-offset shadows, coarse paper fibre, flat misregistered ink, poster pasted and printed too heavily.
Monumental, collective, graphic. No gradients, no soft shadows, no 3D, no text, no Cyrillic, no
lettering.
```

## 6. `pixel` — "cartridge-era CRT"

**Swatches:** ground deep indigo `#10121c` (panel `#1a1d2e`, panel2 `#262a44`), ink `#e8e8f0`,
accent mint `#4ce0b3`. Secondary: cyan `#5bc8ff`, pink `#ff6b97`, violet `#b06bff`.

**Visual language.** "The phone shell stops being glass and becomes a cartridge-era menu screen"; the
desktop is "the same console with a keyboard bolted on. The ground is deep indigo, every plate is a 2 px
frame with a hard, blur-free step under it, the page carries a scanline veil and a 4 px grid … Every
radius, blur, glow, gradient and translucent lamp is switched off." So the medium is **authentic
low-resolution pixel art** — an actual 16-bit console scene, not a 3D render with a pixel filter. Hard
aliased edges, **no anti-aliasing anywhere**, a deliberately limited palette (12–24 colours drawn from
the skin's tokens), and **ordered dithering** to build gradients out of checkered pixels. Composition is
a *scene*, read like a game screen: a horizon or a floor line, a strong silhouette, a big readable
subject, and a 4 px modular grid that the whole image obeys. The ground is deep indigo `#10121c` with
panels `#1a1d2e`/`#262a44`; the accent mint `#4ce0b3` is the interactive/highlight colour; cyan, pink
and violet are the secondary incident lights. Lighting is diegetic and chunky — a few flat-shaded
clusters of pixels, rim light as a single-pixel line. The music is the **scene itself**: a chiptune stage
with waveform architecture, an arcade cabinet, a boombox on a bedroom floor, a synthesizer as a game
object, a night city with a radio tower — but abstracted, never literal text or UI. Texture: a **CRT
scanline veil over the whole image**, a faint 4 px pixel grid in the ink at very low alpha, a soft
phosphor bloom around the brightest pixels only, and a subtle vignette. **Avoid:** anti-aliasing, smooth
curves, gradients without dithering, more than ~24 colours, 3D perspective rendering, glossy highlight,
photo-realism, blur, and any sprite-style lettering or HUD text.

**Prompt template**
```
Authentic 16-bit pixel art, low-resolution console graphics, square album cover, scene: {GENRE_SUBJECT},
expressing the sound of {GENRE} — {GENRE_CHARACTER}. Strict pixel grid with hard aliased edges and no
anti-aliasing, limited palette of about twenty colours drawn from deep indigo (#10121c), panel indigo
(#1a1d2e, #262a44), pale ink (#e8e8f0), mint accent (#4ce0b3) with cyan (#5bc8ff), pink (#ff6b97) and
violet (#b06bff) incident light. Ordered dithering for all shading, flat chunky clusters of pixels, a
single-pixel rim light, strong readable silhouette, flat-shaded scene with a clear horizon or floor line,
CRT scanline veil over the whole image, soft phosphor bloom on the brightest pixels only, faint vignette.
Nostalgic, crisp, deliberately low-resolution. No anti-aliasing, no smooth curves, no lettering, no HUD
text, no UI.
```
